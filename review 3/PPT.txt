SLIDE 3 — THE PROBLEM
----------------------------------------------------------------
Tag     : THE PROBLEM
Heading : The problem I looked at

• What was going wrong: Forest fires are increasing globally; CNN models lack
  global scene context; ViTs need large labelled datasets — both fail on small
  fire benchmarks

• Where I studied it: Kaggle fire dataset (phylake1337, 999 images);
  Google Colab (NVIDIA T4 GPU); literature from IEEE Xplore & arXiv (2019–2023)

• What others tried: CNN detectors (FireNet 2019, ResNet-50 2020,
  EfficientNet-B0 2021) achieve ~97–98% accuracy but no global reasoning;
  no published hybrid CNN+Transformer for fire detection with explainability

• Why it matters now: Climate change is accelerating wildfire frequency;
  satellite imaging has 1–2 hour latency; deep learning on camera feeds
  can respond in seconds


----------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 4 — THE GOAL
----------------------------------------------------------------
Tag     : THE GOAL
Heading : What I set out to do

• I wanted to find out: whether combining EfficientNet-B4 (CNN) with a
  4-layer Transformer Encoder produces a model that outperforms standalone
  CNN and ViT baselines on a small real-world fire dataset

• I also wanted to understand: how CNN local features and Transformer global
  attention complement each other, using Grad-CAM and CLS-token attention maps
  to explain model decisions

• And to suggest: a deployment-ready plan — ViT for immediate safety-critical
  use; Hybrid for scaled-up training — with a phased implementation timeline
  and risk mitigation strategy

• This connects to SDG 13 (Climate Action) and SDG 15 (Life on Land):
  earlier detection reduces megafire CO₂ emissions and protects
  biodiversity-critical forest ecosystems

SLIDE 5 — THE APPROACH
----------------------------------------------------------------
Tag     : THE APPROACH
Heading : How I went about it

• I gathered information from: Kaggle fire dataset (999 images), 10
  peer-reviewed papers (IEEE Xplore, arXiv, Google Scholar), pretrained
  weights via timm (EfficientNet-B4 / ImageNet-1K; ViT-B/16 / ImageNet-21K)

• I analysed it using: PyTorch 2.x, scikit-learn metrics (Accuracy,
  Precision, Recall, F1, AUC-ROC), Grad-CAM via pytorch-grad-cam,
  CLS-token attention maps via forward hooks, matplotlib/seaborn visualisations

• Plan across three reviews: R1 — problem framing & architecture design;
  R2 — model training, evidence analysis & interpretability;
  R3 — final evaluation, recommendations, deployment plan

• What I had to adjust: added two-phase training after single-phase
  overfitted; added class-weighted loss after model biased toward fire class;
  reduced batch size 32→16 for ViT/Hybrid due to GPU memory

SLIDE 6 — THE EVIDENCE
----------------------------------------------------------------
Tag     : THE EVIDENCE
Heading : What the data told me

• What I looked at: 152 held-out test images evaluated across CNN
  (EfficientNet-B4), ViT-B/16, and Hybrid models; confusion matrices,
  ROC curves, Grad-CAM maps, CLS-token attention overlays, training curves

• How I analysed it: sklearn classification_report & roc_auc_score;
  seaborn confusion heatmaps; pytorch-grad-cam on last EfficientNet-B4
  conv block; 7×7 CLS attention grids bicubically upsampled to 224×224

• The pattern that stood out: ViT achieves perfect AUC-ROC (1.000)
  and 100% Recall (0 missed fires); all 3 models exceed 98% accuracy;
  Hybrid ranked 3rd due to insufficient training data — not a design flaw

• What it means: dataset scale is the binding constraint; ViT is the
  optimal immediate deployment; Hybrid unlocks at 5,000+ images as
  its global attention can fully train

SLIDE 7 — THE THINKING BEHIND IT
----------------------------------------------------------------
Tag     : THE THINKING BEHIND IT
Heading : The ideas that guided me

• The theories leaned on: CNN local feature extraction (LeCun 1989;
  EfficientNet, Tan & Le 2019); Transformer self-attention (Vaswani 2017;
  ViT, Dosovitskiy 2021); Hybrid design (CoAtNet, Dai 2021);
  Transfer Learning (Pan & Yang 2010); Grad-CAM (Selvaraju 2017)

• How they explain the real case: Grad-CAM confirms localised
  flame-boundary activations (CNN theory validated); CLS attention
  confirms global smoke+fire context capture (Transformer theory validated);
  two-phase training preserved ImageNet features (catastrophic forgetting
  theory validated)

• How they shaped the recommendation: since Transformer scales with data
  (theory), Hybrid advantage expected at 5,000+ images — driving phased
  deployment plan; since explainability is non-negotiable in safety systems,
  dual Grad-CAM + attention maps included in the deployment spec

SLIDE 8 — THE ANSWER
----------------------------------------------------------------
Tag     : THE ANSWER
Heading : What I recommend

• My main recommendation: deploy ViT-B/16 immediately (perfect Recall,
  AUC=1.000 on test set); collect data to 5,000+ images; transition to
  Hybrid CNN+Transformer (architecturally superior, dual-interpretability,
  scalable)

• Why I believe it works: ViT — 99.34% accuracy, 100% Recall, 0 missed
  fires, AUC=1.000 on 152-image test set; Hybrid — Grad-CAM (local) +
  CLS attention (global) provide two independent explanation channels
  unavailable in ViT

• What would change if adopted: detection latency drops from 1–2 hours
  (satellite) to <2 minutes; monitoring extends to remote forests;
  false alarm rate reduces as training data grows (currently 1–2 per
  152 images)

• What it does not solve (openly): video/temporal fire tracking,
  multi-class labels (fire/smoke/smouldering), UAV/edge deployment on
  Jetson hardware — all defined as future work with specific next steps

SLIDE 11 — WRAPPING UP
----------------------------------------------------------------
Tag     : WRAPPING UP
Heading : What I learned

• In short: forest fires need sub-minute detection; CNN (98.68%),
  ViT (99.34%), Hybrid (98.03%) all achieved high accuracy on 152-image
  test; ViT = zero missed fires; Hybrid needs scale to reach its ceiling —
  deploy ViT now, Hybrid later

• What this case taught me: dataset scale is more decisive than architecture
  on small benchmarks; interpretability is a deployment prerequisite, not
  an add-on; complementary explanations (Grad-CAM + attention) are stronger
  than either alone

• What I could not cover: video/temporal fire tracking, multi-class labels
  (fire/smoke/smouldering), edge deployment on UAV/Jetson hardware,
  geo-diverse validation across climate zones

• Where this goes next: D-Fire (21,527 images) to validate Hybrid advantage;
  self-supervised pretraining (MAE/DINO) for fire domain; knowledge
  distillation for edge inference; federated learning across distributed
  camera networks
